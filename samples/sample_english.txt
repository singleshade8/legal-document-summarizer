IN THE COURT OF THE JUDICIAL MAGISTRATE FIRST CLASS, COIMBATORE
S.T.C. No. 1187/2022
M/s Sri Vinayaga Traders, represented by its Proprietor Mr. K. Selvam .... Complainant
vs
Mr. R. Anand Kumar .... Accused
Date of Judgment: 14/03/2024

JUDGMENT

1. The complainant has filed this complaint under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 against the accused for dishonour of a cheque for Rs. 4,50,000.
2. The case of the complainant is that the accused purchased hardware goods worth Rs. 4,50,000 from the complainant's shop between January and March 2022 and promised to pay the amount within sixty days.
3. In discharge of this liability, the accused issued cheque No. 004521 dated 10/06/2022 drawn on the State Bank of India, Race Course Road Branch, Coimbatore.
4. When the complainant presented the cheque for collection on 14/06/2022, it was returned unpaid by the bank on 16/06/2022 with the endorsement "funds insufficient".
5. The complainant issued a statutory legal notice on 28/06/2022 demanding payment within fifteen days of receipt, and the notice was served on the accused on 01/07/2022.
6. The accused neither paid the amount nor sent any reply, and the complainant filed the present complaint on 29/07/2022 within the period of limitation.
7. The complainant examined himself as PW1 and marked Exhibits P1 to P9, which include the cheque, the bank return memo, the legal notice and the postal acknowledgement.
8. The learned counsel for the accused contended that the cheque was given only as a security for a loan transaction and that no legally enforceable debt existed on the date of the cheque.
9. It was further submitted by the accused that the signature on the invoices was not his and that the complainant had misused a blank signed cheque.
10. The accused examined himself as DW1 but did not produce any document to substantiate the alleged loan transaction or the plea of misuse of the cheque.
11. The learned counsel for the complainant argued that the accused has admitted his signature on the cheque and that the statutory presumption under Sections 118 and 139 of the Act therefore operates in favour of the complainant.
12. This Court has considered the oral and documentary evidence and the submissions made by both sides.
13. It is not disputed that the cheque belongs to the account of the accused and bears his signature, which he admitted during cross-examination.
14. Once the signature is admitted, the Court is bound to presume that the cheque was issued for a legally enforceable debt, and the burden shifts upon the accused to rebut this presumption by a preponderance of probabilities.
15. The Court finds that the accused has not led any cogent evidence to rebut the presumption, and the plea of a blank signed security cheque was raised for the first time only in the course of cross-examination.
16. The Court further finds that the complainant has proved the dishonour of the cheque, the service of the statutory notice and the failure of the accused to pay within the stipulated period.
17. All the ingredients of the offence under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 are therefore established beyond reasonable doubt.
18. Accordingly, the accused Mr. R. Anand Kumar is found guilty and is convicted for the offence punishable under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881.
19. Considering that the offence is of a commercial nature, the accused is sentenced to undergo simple imprisonment for six months and to pay compensation of Rs. 5,00,000 to the complainant under Section 357(3) of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 within sixty days.
20. In default of payment of the compensation, the accused shall undergo further simple imprisonment for three months.
21. The bail bond of the accused shall remain in force for a period of thirty days to enable him to prefer an appeal.

Judicial Magistrate First Class
Coimbatore
